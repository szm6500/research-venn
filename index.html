Research Venn

A small interaction for explaining a research identity through the relationship between areas of work—not only a chronological list of papers.

Why I made this

I wanted a clearer way to communicate where research sits and how earlier work connects to the areas it is moving toward. A CV can show output; it is less good at showing the logic between that output.

This is deliberately a working study, not a finished product.

Explore the research map

How the pointer interaction works

The interaction uses direction, not a click on a pre-drawn overlap. First, select one circle as the primary foundation. Then move the pointer around inside it: which side of the circle you are on decides which neighbor it is compared with, and a short arc on the rim shows that side.

heading = angle(primary.center -> pointer)
for each neighbor: gap = angle between heading and angle(primary.center -> neighbor.center)
secondary = neighbor with the smallest gap
intersection = findPair(primary.id, secondary.id)

A small dead zone around the center and a few degrees of stickiness at the edges between sides keep it from flickering. Keyboard: Enter selects, arrow keys change side. Touch: tap a side.

Make your own

The circles, colors and shared terms all come from a plain Markdown file, so there is no geometry to edit. Add a # Circle line and the layout adjusts.

Bottom-up workflow

  1. Start with publications or projects you already have.
  2. Assign each item meaningful tags in JSON.
  3. Group the same terms into research intersections.
  4. Use the resulting vocabulary to see patterns, gaps, and connections.
  5. Place the interaction wherever it helps explain the work.

The Venn diagram is only one possible view. The useful artifact is the connected vocabulary: JSON keeps a conceptual term linked to actual evidence.
